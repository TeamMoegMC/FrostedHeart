# Hot Spring

- Status: `Partial` (source/contact runtime validated; spring gain calibration and gameplay/client validation pending)
- Last verified: `2026-10-21` against source and cached Forge/Create/IE sources
- Scope: hot spring world fluid, player contact heat exchange, collection limits
- Code anchors: `FHFluids.HOT_SPRING`, `FHFluids.HOT_SPRING_TAG`, `HotSpringBlock.pickupBlock`, `PlayerTemperatureComputation.updatePlayer`, `PlayerThermalModel.preparePart`, `PlayerTemperatureUpdate.isSoaked`, `OpenEndedPipeMixin_HotSpring`, `FluidDrainingBehaviourMixin_HotSpring`, `UtilsMixin_HotSpring`, `GlassBottleItemMixin_HotSpring`, `LiquidBlockInteractionMixin_HotSpring`

`frostedheart:hot_spring` is a world fluid with source and flowing states. Registrate registers `frostedheart:hot_spring` as the source fluid and block, and `frostedheart:flowing_hot_spring` as the flowing fluid. It uses vanilla water textures with a light blue tint. Its Forge `FluidType` temperature is `311 K` (an approximate 38 C metadata value); the player contact boundary is exactly the source default `38 C` in `PlayerThermalModel.HOT_SPRING_TEMPERATURE_C`. The metadata does not register a world heat source or set material energy.

The spring uses Registrate's `FluidTypeFactory` overload and an explicit `ForgeFlowingFluid.Source` factory. Source, flowing fluid, and world states share the same registered `frostedheart:hot_spring` fluid type instance. This identity is required by Forge's entity-height map. The existing `FHRegistrate.standardColoredWater` helper supplies fresh unregistered fluid type instances and is unsuitable for this contact-dependent world fluid; the spring's registration fixes its own boundary without changing unrelated fluids.

The fluid flows with water's default slope, level decrease, and tick rate. `FluidType.canConvertToSource(false)` prevents adjacent sources from creating another source. The fluid and block have no bucket item: registration uses `noBucket()` plus an explicit `ForgeFlowingFluid.Properties.bucket(() -> Items.AIR)` supplier for both variants. This avoids Registrate's otherwise unresolved default `hot_spring_bucket` lookup when another mod queries fluid metadata. `HotSpringBlock.pickupBlock` returns an empty stack and leaves the block in place. Both source and flowing fluids belong to `minecraft:water` for ordinary water-like entity behavior and to `frostedheart:hot_spring` for explicit identification. The source and flowing states have no ice phase recipe. Decorative `frostedheart:hydrothermal_rock` beneath generated springs supplies no heat.

Forge records modded fluid contact in the entity's `FluidType` height map. `PlayerTemperatureComputation.updatePlayer` reads `player.getFluidTypeHeight(FHFluids.HOT_SPRING.get().getFluidType()) / player.getBbHeight()`; ordinary water continues to use `getFluidHeight(FluidTags.WATER)`. The latter is Forge's vanilla water-type height and excludes the spring. Each body part uses its existing vertical immersion band. Lava has first contact precedence, then hot spring, ordinary water, powder snow, and air. Where the two water heights overlap, the spring receives the overlapping part once; ordinary water uses only the remaining band fraction. Contact uses the existing water coefficient `100 W/(m2*K)`, water exchange multiplier `0.51`, tissue resistance, clothing insulation, and water resistance. The spring alone additionally multiplies conductance by `PlayerThermalModel.HOT_SPRING_EXCHANGE_GAIN` (provisional source default `64`, pending calibration). This changes heat-transfer speed while retaining the 38 C boundary and energy integration; contact never directly sets body temperature. Ordinary water retains its existing coefficient and multiplier.

A source with air above has a surface height of `8/9` block. Standing on its floor at a 1.8-block body height yields an immersion ratio near `0.494`: feet are fully immersed, legs about `86%`, hands about `31%`, and torso/head have no direct water contact. Core recovery therefore also depends on leg weighting, internal body exchange, food, clothing, and exposed cold air. The spring gain is a dedicated gameplay balance parameter, not an increased water temperature or a world heat source. The desired fast recovery remains a gameplay validation target; numerical fixtures measure the chosen gain before it is treated as settled balance.

`PlayerTemperatureUpdate.isSoaked` explicitly includes spring `FluidType` contact because `player.isInWater()` alone only recognizes vanilla water here. Soaking refreshes the existing Wet effect, with the existing longer duration when armor is equipped. Leaving spring water removes its contact heat exchange on the next body update; Wet persists and continues the existing exposed-air evaporation path. The fluid type enables swimming and extinguishing through Forge's normal entity hooks.

Known Create world extraction paths are blocked at `OpenEndedPipe.removeFluidFromSpace` and `FluidDrainingBehaviour.pullNext`. IE's world-drain helper `Utils.drainFluidBlock` is blocked for spring fluid, including simulation. IE's separate infinite pump path also requires `canConvertToSource`, which is false. Vanilla `BottleItem.use` excludes spring when checking its water tag. The optional Supplementaries `LiquidBlockInteraction.getProvidedFluid` hook prevents its faucet from offering spring directly from a source. These hooks prevent extraction from a placed spring into the common transfer networks; they do not cover arbitrary external code that creates a `FluidStack` without collecting a spring block. Pack-specific transfer paths require separate verification.

Validation anchors (test definitions; results must be reported separately):

- `PlayerThermalModelHotSpringTest` checks shallow, mixed, and ordinary-water boundaries, including the actual `8/9` source surface height.
- `HotSpringGameTests` checks actual Forge contact, source/flow tags, bucket pickup, Create open pipe, IE world drain, natural flow, the two-source replication case, and flow recession after sources are removed.
- `HotSpringPlayerGameTests` sends actual spring/water/mixed entity contacts through `PlayerTemperatureComputation.updatePlayer`, exercises the Wet regulation event and clothing duration, checks Wet after leaving, and checks deep-water swimming and extinguishing.
- Its recovery fixture runs `preparePart` and `integrateBody` for 120 real seconds at source-default `temperatureChangeRate=1` and the companion pack's `defaultconfigs/frostedheart-server.toml` default `0.5` (`8` / `4` physiological seconds per real second). Inputs are -20 C air, initial uniform body 35 C, 50% relative humidity, no wind, no equipment heating, and measured one-source-block immersion. It compares gains `1, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128` and logs core temperature at 30, 60, and 120 seconds for fed, hungry, coat-only, and coat-plus-pants cases. Test raw attributes: coat insulation `500`, pants insulation `100`, each water resistance `0.5`, passed through normal equipment weighting. The fed case assumes food remains available throughout; this fixture does not simulate resource consumption or a live player's complete tick lifecycle. A separate passive-contact check verifies that accelerated contact itself cannot push any body part beyond 38 C.

Client appearance, actual equipment/air recovery cadence, bottle/faucet interactions, and Create hose pulley behavior still need in-game verification.
