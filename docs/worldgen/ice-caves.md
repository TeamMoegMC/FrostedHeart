# Ice Caves

- Status: `Partial` (source and pack data reviewed; initial and tuned ores sampled conditionally; spring frequency and final balance await verification)
- Last verified: `2026-10-21`
- Scope: `the_winter_rescue:ice_caves` new terrain, surface ice, crystal clusters, recessed hot springs, and local ore additions
- Code anchors: `FHFeatures.ICE_CAVE_SURFACE`, `FHFeatures.ICE_CAVE_SPRING`, `FHFeatures.CAVE_PYROLUSITE`, `IceCaveSpace.isWide`, `IceCaveSurfaceFeature`, `IceCaveSpringFeature`, `CavePyrolusiteFeature`
- Data anchors: `kubejs/data/the_winter_rescue/worldgen/biome/ice_caves.json`, `kubejs/data/the_winter_rescue/worldgen/placed_feature/cave/ice_*.json`

The pack attaches the features only to `the_winter_rescue:ice_caves`. They run during new chunk generation; they do not retroactively decorate old chunks. `IceCaveSpace` probes placed terrain: an air column with at least five blocks of vertical clearance and at least three cardinal directions clear for four blocks counts as a wide hall. Other valid cave floor points count as narrow passages. `nearWideAir` accepts any floor with a sturdy upper face, including ore and ice; earlier ore placement therefore does not change the classification merely by replacing floor material. Landscape and basin searches retain their narrower stone/deepslate/tuff predicate. This describes nearby space, not the carver or noise function that created it. The landscape features read a bounded neighborhood and keep their writes inside the generating chunk; vanilla ore placement follows its own normal vein boundary rules.

`frostedheart:ice_cave_surface` paints small exposed stone/deepslate/tuff floor and wall patches with ordinary ice. A random third of floor candidates remain stone, providing passable shore and rest spots. Wide halls can gain `frostedheart:glowstone_crystal` replacing ice, with `frostedheart:ice_crystal` immediately adjacent in air. Wide halls can also gain a one-block water pocket under `frostedheart:thin_ice`. The block itself follows the existing thin-ice rules; no new cracking rule is introduced.

`frostedheart:ice_cave_spring` checks up to nine nearby floor columns at Y<=0. Candidate centers stay at local chunk coordinates 5..10; each column searches up to eight blocks vertically. A candidate needs wide space at its floor or one block above it. The basin checks 37 columns within x²+z²<=10: each needs a stone/deepslate/tuff bed two blocks below the walking height, air one block above it, and only air or stone/deepslate/tuff at the walking height and immediately below. This allows one-block bumps and dips while rejecting deeper unsupported cavities. Placement clears the small bumps and fills shallow rim dips with stone, then recesses 13 `frostedheart:hot_spring` sources within x²+z²<=4 below the dry shore. The pool bed is 75% `frostedheart:hydrothermal_rock` and 25% calcite in expectation; it is cosmetic and provides no heat. Local crystal markers at the pool and within four blocks follow one open cardinal approach. Glowstone markers replace newly placed or existing ice, then place an ordinary ice crystal adjacent. This is a short hint only; no continuous route is guaranteed. Actual fluid warmth and transport rules belong to the climate documentation and fluid implementation.

The pack retains generic pyrite `the_winter_rescue:pyrite_ore` via `#minecraft:is_overworld` at 8 attempts x size 8 over Y -63..0. Ice caves add `ice_pyrite_ore` at 13 x 30 over Y -48..35. Both use only `#minecraft:stone_ore_replaceables` and `#minecraft:deepslate_ore_replaceables`, never ice. The current vanilla plus pack stone tag includes stone, granite, diorite, andesite and Create limestone; the deepslate tag includes deepslate, tuff and basalt. These are ore host tags, separate from the landscape feature's narrower stone/deepslate/tuff predicate. Pyrolusite pairs each of those two attempt domains exactly: `ice_pyrolusite_ore_generic` is 8 x 8 over Y -63..0 and `ice_pyrolusite_ore` is 13 x 30 over Y -48..35, with the same stone/deepslate predicates. At each attempt, `CavePyrolusiteFeature` admits 1/2 in nearby wide space or 1/4 elsewhere. Therefore the configured expected budget is 227 versus local pyrite 454 in wide space, or 113.5 versus 454 in narrow space, before replacement losses. Those are expectation values, not measured ore-block counts or a per-room guarantee.

Other ice-cave additions use the vanilla ore algorithm: zinc 16 x 20 over Y -48..35, lead 20 x 20 over Y -48..0, and silver 16 x 20 over Y -48..0. Adding the generic ordinary budgets yields zinc 416 versus the hydrothermal ordinary budget 216, lead 400 versus hydrothermal 40, and silver 380 versus hydrothermal 80. Hydrothermal geodes add lead, pyrite, and silver outside that ordinary budget; actual total ore-block counts can differ, and the present values are provisional pending natural chunk sampling. See [Mineral Generation Estimates](../worldgen-mineral-generation-estimate.md) for the source inventory and limits of the Q=count x size metric.

Provisional placement parameters are 32 cave-surface attempts per chunk at Y -48..40. Springs use four candidate attempts per chunk, each passing a 1/2 rarity filter, for two attempts per chunk in expectation at Y -40..0. This replaced the earlier one-attempt-per-six-chunks setting after two rounds yielded no pools in the sampled interiors. Spatial validation still rejects candidates, so attempt frequency is not actual pool spacing. Actual cave coverage, pool interval, exposed ore totals, and wide/narrow ratio require broader natural terrain measurement and in-game inspection before balance is considered verified.

The existing biome-temperature recipe remains an additive `-5 C`, not a fixed cave temperature or entry requirement. Depth, climate and the current thermal runtime still determine actual air temperature. Springs supply a player contact medium only and do not raise this biome or the surrounding world temperature.

Later cave-surface placement preserves rock cells touching spring water (including diagonals), so it does not repaint the immediate stone shore with ice. Practical pool spacing remains subject to natural-terrain sampling. `IceCaveFeatureGameTests` exercises the real features on synthetic terrain, including uneven floors, unsupported beds, supported ice-only crystal placement, repeated surface passes around the shore, and positive/negative chunk edges. These geometry tests are separate from natural noise terrain sampling.

## Initial conditional terrain sample

Three seeds (`111`, `222`, `333`) each generated a 4 x 4 chunk interior plus a fully generated one-chunk border for each of two fixed-biome dimensions. The dimensions used the companion pack noise, carver, feature and applicable generic modifiers, with its IE/Create configuration. This measures actual generated blocks conditioned on a fixed biome; it does not measure normal-preset biome occurrence. The initial sampler completed successfully (`build/ice-caves-natural-sample.log`); raw results were preserved in `build/ice-cave-natural-samples-initial.json`.

Across 48 interior chunks per biome, the initial ice-cave counts were pyrite 36,270; zinc 13,270; lead 4,187; silver 1,275. Hydrothermal counts including geodes were 38,136; 10,736; 12,185; 12,289 respectively. There were 8 ice crystals and no spring sources in the ice-cave interiors. These results motivated the current increased ore attempts and local basin leveling; they are not results for the revised settings.

## Tuned ore sample and spring diagnostics

The same three seeds and interior areas were regenerated after the ore and basin changes, with the earlier one-per-six-chunks spring frequency still in place. Results in `build/ice-cave-natural-samples-tuned.json` totalled ice-cave pyrite 50,831; pyrolusite 13,499; zinc 25,101; lead 26,573; silver 20,926. Hydrothermal totals including geodes were pyrite 38,133; zinc 10,737; lead 12,185; silver 12,289. Each individual seed had more ice-cave pyrite, zinc, lead and silver than its paired hydrothermal sample. Silver remained below each of ice-cave pyrite, zinc and lead. These are actual conditional block counts for the current ore settings, not estimates from Q or normal-preset resource frequency.

That round still had no spring sources in 48 ice-cave interior chunks; this alone does not establish that the algorithm cannot place a natural pool. The follow-up sampler first generates terrain to `ChunkStatus.CARVERS`, enumerates the four possible clamped x/z anchor pairs at every source height -40..0, and calls the same `IceCaveSpringFeature.findBasin` predicate without placing blocks. It records distinct viable basin positions and successful probe origins before any feature decoration, then completes the neighboring features and counts the final interior blocks. Earlier features can change candidate terrain before the spring's own stage, so these diagnostics describe pre-feature opportunities rather than guaranteed placement successes. The increased spring frequency remains provisional pending that run.
