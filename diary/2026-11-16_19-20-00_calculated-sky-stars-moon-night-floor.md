## Completed

- **Stars.** While the calculated sky is drawn, vanilla's stars take `sqrt(exp(-d / sin 45) * max(0, 1 - z / Z))` on all four arguments of their `setShaderColor` call (ordinal `3` in `renderSky`), with `d` the drawn ice optical depth, `z` the displayed luminance of the sky overhead, and `Z` the new setting `starTwilightDisplay` (`0.25`). Vanilla passes the star brightness as color and alpha, so the light added goes as its square, hence the square root. Vanilla's dimming by the sun's elevation stays.
- **Moon sprite.** The color of the sun and moon call (ordinal `2`) takes, per channel, the display of the sun's square's radiance at the moon's elevation, blended between the depths as the shader does, times the shader's horizon fade. The alpha stays `1 - rainLevel`. So the moon dims and reddens toward the horizon, dims evenly through ice, and is gone below the horizon. In snow a moon above about `35 degrees` stays near full under vanilla's alpha `0.2` and a lower one dims, as the sun's square does.
- **Night floor.** New setting `nightSkyFloor` (`0.04`, a displayed luminance): a floor tinted like moonlight, applied to the tone mapped value as `1 - (1 - t)(1 - f)` in the shader and in the fog color, so the sky and the fog agree at the horizon. The daylight on the terrain does not take it.
- Both factors are computed once per frame after the draw (`CalculatedSkyRenderer.updateNightSky`) and used only while the sky was drawn in the frame. The hooks are `@ModifyArg` handlers in `LevelRendererMixin`, which allocate nothing, instead of the plan's `@Inject` at the return of `ClientLevel.getStarBrightness`, whose returnable callback is allocated on every call.
- Docs: a new section "Stars, the moon sprite, and the night floor", the hooks, the shader's floor, the fog color, `SkyFrame`, the settings table, the scope and anchors, and the known limits.

## Decisions

- The moon sprite takes option (a), decided with empireu on `2026-11-16`: the tone mapped transmittance of the moon's disc as the sun's square has it, not `exp(-d / max(sin e, 0.05))`, which would hide the moon in snow, and not a factor only outside snow. It is per channel, so the moon also reddens low, from the air, since the ice is gray. It takes neither `moonlightBrightness` nor the phase, since the sprite's texture shows the phase and should stay as bright as vanilla's in clear air.
- The sky overhead for the stars counts the ice's tumbling crystals at the angle between the zenith and the light, but at least `45 degrees`, so a moon overhead does not dim every star by its own glare.
- The floor is tinted by the moonlight's blue shift, so it follows empireu's choice of the night's color.
