## Completed

- **Unit checked.** A radiance of `1` is a sun of irradiance `1` per band, about `128000 lux` above the air, so `1.28e5 cd/m2` (`SkyAdaptation.CANDELA_PER_RADIANCE`). Sunlit snow overhead then has about `32600 cd/m2` and the clear sky overhead at sun `30 degrees` about `5700`, both as real ones.
- **Adapted exposure** (`SkyAdaptation.exposure`): the key of Krawczyk, Myszkowski, and Seidel 2005 between two limits. The exposure is `exposure` (`6`) while the sky is brighter than `adaptationDayLuminance` (`800 cd/m2`, the clear sky at sun about `2 degrees`), `nightExposure` (`30000`) below `adaptationNightLuminance` (`0.058` after the revision below), and between them `E_d (E_n / E_d)^s`, with `s` the share of `ln(a(L) / L)` between its values at the limits.
- **Luminance the eye adapts to** (`SkyView.adaptationRadiance`, `SkyViewSet.adaptationLuminance`): the sky's diffuse irradiance on level ground over `pi`, with the ground's glow and the ice's light but not the diffraction peak or the light itself, computed with each view on the builder thread and blended per frame like the other view values, times the light's scale.
- **Moon at its real brightness**: `moonlightBrightness` default `2.5e-6`, range up to `1e-4`. `30000 * 2.5e-6 = 6 * 0.0125`, so moonlit nights keep the look empireu tuned on `2026-11-15`, except that a full moon overhead lowers the exposure by about a tenth.
- **Consumers**: `CalculatedSkyRenderer.exposure` gives the adapted exposure to the shader, the fog color, the daylight on the terrain, the zenith used for the star dimming, and the sunlit and moonlit glints. The moon sprite keeps the day exposure (`SkyFrame.dayExposure`, renamed from `exposure`). Lamp glints and light pillars do not use the exposure.
- **Switch** `exposureAdapts` (on): off gives `exposure` under the sun and `nightExposure` under the moon, which is the output from before.
- `nightSkyFloor` stays as a display floor, now described as the gray eyes see in complete darkness: airglow of about `2e-4 cd/m2` would display as about `5e-5` at the night exposure.
- The screenshot manifest prints settings with `Double.toString`, so small values such as the moon's show.
- New harness set `run/sky-shots/adapt.json` (ignored by git): vanilla, adaptation off, and adaptation with day luminances `400`, `800`, and `2000`, for poses west and east at height `200` and one at the run's start facing west, at sun `10` to `-18 degrees`. `150` shots.

## Decisions

- With empireu on `2026-11-16`. Krawczyk's key alone would show the whole day equally bright and every night alike, so it runs between two limits that keep today's day and night. empireu asked that the result not spread too far from the current settings and left the range to Claude: the day limit is the clear sky at sun about `2 degrees` instead of the `5.5` first proposed, and the harness compares `400`, `800`, and `2000`.
- The full moon is to be "factored in a little bit": the night limit sits just below the luminance of a full moon overhead.
- No time course: the exposure follows the outdoor sky only, so caves and houses are not brightened.

## Close

- empireu asked whether the red band was physical. From the air it is not: the fog takes the horizon's color at every height, so all terrain below the horizon takes the glow's color, where real terrain a few hundred blocks away would be lit snow with little light from the air. Vanilla does the same and brighter, `(186, 73, 53)` against `(105, 34, 23)` at sun `-6` from height `200`, measured in the second run's vanilla column.
- empireu found the work drifting, each harness run followed by another patch. Sorted by cause: the brightest-horizon term and the switch at `-15` fix faults the exposure made, the red sheet from the air is the fog's, and the dark dip before the moon is the one-light sky of step 2, moved later by the switch.
- empireu named the bright red band at sun `-6..-8` the weakest part. Cause found: between the limits the exposure grows as `(a(L) / L)^1.25`, so the brightest horizon displays up to `1.5` times brighter at `-6` than at sunset, then four times dimmer by `-12`. A curve holding the display of `L` at `a(L)^0.38` would darken steadily with the same limits. At normal speed one degree takes about `3` seconds (`61.5` ticks), so the brightest red lasts about `6` seconds and the band about `20`. empireu then played a sunset in real time, found it good, and closed the step as it is.
- Docs: the known limits now hold the fog at height, the one-light gap before the moon, the exposure's overshoot with the steady alternative, and the measured terrain light.
