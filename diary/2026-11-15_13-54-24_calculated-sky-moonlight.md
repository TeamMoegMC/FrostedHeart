## Completed

- **The moon lights the sky from the sun's tables.** The tables depend on the light's elevation, the ice depth, and the settings, so the moon, at the elevation `-e` opposite a sun at `e`, uses them as they are. `MoonLight.chooseLight` picks the light every frame: the sun at or above `moonlightSwitchDegrees` (default `-9.5`), the moon below. The sun fades out over the `3 degrees` above the switch and the moon fades in over `moonlightFadeDegrees` (default `4`) below it, both by smoothstep, so the sky is black at the switch.
- **Brightness, phase, and color.** The moon's scale is `moonlightBrightness` (default `0.025` of the sun's irradiance, a gain, since the real `2.5e-6` would not show) times the phase law of Krisciunas and Schaefer 1991 at the phase angle `45 * phase` degrees, times the fade. Its color blends by `moonlightBlueShift` (default `0.5`) from the moon's own color, `(1.20, 1.00, 0.82)` near full by the ROLO model of Kieffer and Stone 2005, to the scotopic blue of Jensen et al. 2000, chromaticity `(0.25, 0.25)` as linear sRGB `(0.706, 0.990, 1.966)`.
- **The kind rule.** `SkyState` records the light kind it was built for, and `isFor` and `SkyBuilder.request` compare it, so a switch starts a build that reuses the halo maps and views on the same grid indices. `SkyLight.radianceScale` is `0` while the state was built for the other kind, so the sky stays black until the build lands.
- **Where the scale goes.** Before the tone map in the shader (`lightScale`, with `sunScale` leaving out the sun's square for the moon), in the fog color (`SkyViewSet.horizonDisplayColor`), and in the daylight (`SkyViewSet.daylightColor`). The old signatures of the two `SkyViewSet` methods stay as the forms for a light at full strength.
- **The terrain keeps vanilla's night light.** The daylight takes `SkyLight.terrainScale`, the sun's scale and `0` for the moon.
- **Docs.** A Moon section in `docs/climate/calculated-sky.md` with the formulas, sources, and the switch measurement, the four settings, the build schedule, the fog color, and the known limits.

## Decisions

- The switch elevation `-9.5` comes from a measurement (below), with the sun's fade above it covering higher exposures.
- The colors changed from the plan's after a literature check that empireu ran with an outside agent (`D:\tmp\night\night-report.md`, outside the repository). The plan's own color `(1.15, 1.00, 0.85)` was too weak. The blue `(1.05, 0.97, 1.27)` that I remembered from Jensen et al. is in neither paper. It is Durand and Dorsey's mild shift, so the end at `1` is now Jensen's full scotopic hue.
- The moon's reddening away from full, to about `(1.27, 1.00, 0.74)` at a quarter, and the opposition surge within a few degrees of full are left out. Quarter moons display nearly black, and the full moon's brightness is a gain set by eye.
- A fade of `0` switches at once, as the setting's comment says.
- The plan said the floor in `daylightColor` keeps the moon off the terrain. A review found the floor only stops darkening, and a full moon high in the sky would light the terrain about twice as bright as vanilla's night light. The renderer now gives the terrain light only the sun's scale, as the plan's decision intends.
- While the sky is not drawn (blindness, darkness, a boss's world fog), no build is requested, so after a switch the fog color stays black until the sky draws again. Blindness and darkness darken vanilla's fog anyway, and the world fog is the dragon's, so this stays.
