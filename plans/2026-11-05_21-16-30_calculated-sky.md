# Calculated Sky From Nanobot And Ice Scattering

- Time: `2026-11-05 21:16:30 +0200`
- Authors: `empireu; Claude (Anthropic, claude-opus-5-5) as implementing agent`
- Status: `in-progress`
- Scope: overworld sky, fog colour, and daylight colour on the client; `content/climate/render/sky`
- Related: `design/lore.md` and `design/world-design.md` (nanobots of titanium dioxide, about `100 nm`), [current behaviour](../docs/climate/calculated-sky.md), [weather rendering](../docs/climate/weather-rendering.md)

## Goal

Calculate the sky from the lore's particles instead of fixed biome colours, when no shader pack is active. One model should eventually drive the sky, the fog colour, and the daylight colour.

## Verified Current State

- Vanilla `LevelRenderer.renderSky` draws a flat-coloured dome from `ClientLevel.getSkyColor`, then the sunrise fan from `DimensionSpecialEffects.getSunriseColor`, then sun, moon, and stars.
- `FogModification.renderFogColors` changes the fog colour only when `prevFogDensity > 0`, so clear-weather fog is vanilla.
- Oculus `1.20.1-1.7.0` and Embeddium `0.3.31` add only `@Inject` hooks to `renderSky` and `getSunriseColor`, so an inject after the dome and an override of `getSunriseColor` do not conflict with them.
- FrostedHeart does not use `IrisApi` elsewhere. Oculus is `compileOnly` and `clientRuntimeOnly`.

## Decisions

- Method: Hillaire 2020 lookup tables, plus a term for light reflected between snow and atmosphere. Its formula is in the docs. Single scattering in the style of Unity's The Blacksmith was compared offline and rejected as too dark and too olive over snow.
- Tables are built on the CPU, on `Util.backgroundExecutor()`. The Java model was checked against an offline Monte Carlo reference, and a GLSL port would need checking again. This also avoids float render targets.
- Vanilla draws the sun, moon, and stars, so only the dome is replaced. This keeps vanilla's exits, moon phases, and star fade without copying them.
- On `2026-11-07` empireu chose to replace vanilla's sun with one the shader draws, after vanilla's square core and glow showed inside the sky's own glow. It has the size and place of vanilla's core, and each texel takes the transmittance of its own elevation, rather than one color for the whole square, so the horizon cuts it off at sunset. empireu asked for it to glide smoothly. Edges on sky texels made the square seem to change shape as it moved, so its outline follows screen pixels while its color follows the sky texels. The moon and stars stay vanilla.
- The sky toggles at runtime by config and command. Testing compares on and off in one game instance at a frozen time of day.
- Default off until the maintainers decide.
- On `2026-11-07` empireu chose a halo display from hexagonal ice crystals over the three-part ice phase, with the old ring model dropped. Halos come from formulas over tables that the offline preview ray traced, shipped as raw data in `assets/frostedheart/datasets/ice_halos` after the dataset convention of ElectricalAge2. The generator stays in the preview for now. It may later move into the mod so it can run on demand.
- The whole sky is drawn on square texels fixed to the sky, like the sun's texture (`texelDegrees`, `0` for smooth). Texels fixed to the screen were rejected, because thin arcs would crawl as the camera turns. The default is `0.5 degrees`, which empireu chose after `1 degree` looked coarse in game. The halo map has `0.5 degree` bins, so finer texels show halos at that detail.
- Ice optical depth stays at `0.1` for now, to compare with `0.3` in game.
- On `2026-11-07` empireu asked for a screenshot harness, so that one command in a fresh world takes the whole comparison set. It sat behind a constant in the Java code, `CalculatedSkyScreenshots.ENABLED`, which gated its subcommands and with them everything else of the harness. On `2026-11-11` empireu chose to keep the harness and to gate the whole `/calculatedSky` command instead, with `CalculatedSkyClientCommand.ENABLED`, which is `true` while the sky is developed on this branch and set to `false` before a pull request. Without the command the sky follows the client config alone.
- On `2026-11-07` empireu chose to take both the color and the brightness of daylight on the terrain from the model, through the sky's own exposure, rather than the color alone. Measured on snow, the model's light is nearly white all day, and vanilla's dims much more slowly than the model's as the sun sets, which left bright snow under a dark sky. Vanilla's night sky light stays as a floor, so nights are never darker than vanilla, and rain fades back to vanilla's light.
- On `2026-11-08` empireu agreed that the ice optical depth should follow the weather, with halos ahead of snow as cirrostratus comes before a front. The proposed law: `0.02..0.15` on clear days by the day's humidity, rising over the `24` climate hours before forecast snow to `0.4` at `12` hours, `1` at `6` hours, and `3` when the snow starts, held during snow, and back to the clear day value within about `6` climate hours after it. Changes of depth crossfade between tables, because small jumps are easy to see. Diamond dust, the ice of clear, calm, very cold air near the ground, comes after the sky as its own feature.
- On `2026-11-08` empireu asked to delta scale the ice before the law: the diffracted half of the ice's scattering counts as transmitted for the sun's beam, the light between scatterings, and the sky seen through ice, while the sun's square keeps the full extinction and the glow around it is still drawn.
- On `2026-11-08` empireu found too much light on the ground under thick ice and asked for a fix of the brightness only, without lower clouds for gloom before snow. The multiple scattering table now keeps only `1 - g` of the ice, lit through the same reduced extinction, which the preview's path tracer preferred over also reducing the snow's bounce.

## Steps

1. Done in the working tree: sky dome, config, client command, GameTests, docs. After the first in-game run on `2026-11-05`, the ice phase function was split into a diffraction peak, a broad lobe, and the ring, because one lobe with asymmetry `0.8` filled the inside of the halo with glow. Snow now fades the sky to flat grey, because the halo stayed visible through snowfall.
2. Done in the working tree on `2026-11-06`: fog colour from the model's horizon colour outside snow, so terrain fades into the sky. Comparison screenshots showed a cold band under the warm sunset sky, because removing the sunrise fan also removed vanilla's orange fog tint, and a band in snow, because the sky faded to a grey that differed from the pack's snow fog. The sky in snow now fades toward the snow fog instead.
3. Done in the working tree on `2026-11-07`: ice crystal halos (22 and 46 degree halos, sun dogs, parhelic circle, tangent arcs, circumzenithal arc, Parry arcs) and the sky on texels fixed to the sky. A halo map is built on one background thread every `0.5 degrees` of sun elevation, and the two maps around the sun are blended by its exact elevation. Its sampling was made dense enough for `0.5 degree` texels, about `0.45 s` per map in the preview instead of `0.2 s`, after empireu saw the arcs break into colored dots at `0.5` and `0.25 degrees`. A compute shader was considered and set aside: macOS has no OpenGL 4.3, the CPU needs the map for the ground light, and the GameTests have no GL. Later that day the build was made about `2.65` times faster without changing the halos, mostly by dropping repeated trigonometry in the ray tracers: about `82 ms` per map for the mod's own code, measured outside the game on one thread, against `218 ms` before. Merging ray tracer outputs that leave in the same direction then brought a map to about `73 ms`, and working out the view rays once per setting brought a sky view from about `28` to `15.5 ms`, with the same results. Faster sine, cosine, and exponential approximations and 32 bit floats were measured and left out, at about `2 ms` and no gain per map. Then the shader drew its own sun and vanilla's was hidden while the calculated sky is drawn. With the day cycle running, the sky around a low sun changed visibly at every new sky view, so the two views around the sun are now blended like the halo maps.
4. Done in the working tree on `2026-11-07`: daylight color and brightness through `IForgeDimensionSpecialEffects.adjustLightmapColors`, from the ground radiance that each sky view already holds, so nothing new is built.
5. Done on `2026-11-08`: ice optical depth that follows the weather. A probe outside the game showed the halos peak at depth `0.3..0.5` and wash out by `2..5`, and that even steps put one or two samples in the ice layer, which left bands across the sky above depth `0.3`. Done first, committed as `25b29b63e`: ray steps across the ice band and multiple scattering rows concentrated in it. Done second, on empireu's go-ahead, and seen in game at depths `0.1` and `0.5` the same day: delta scaled ice, after the `v1` set at depth `0.5` showed a dim orange sun and a dark gray sky and snow at sunrise and sunset, and transmittance rows concentrated in the ice band, which the check of the delta scaling found too coarse. Done third: the law from the client's forecast, with tables on a grid of `22` depths and the two around the drawn depth blended, and the multiple scattering table with ice reduced by its forward scattering. See [the diary entry](../diary/2026-11-08_19-01-31_calculated-sky-weather-law.md).
6. Done on `2026-11-11`: a refactor of the sky package before new passes and settings, with nothing drawn or computed differently. `SkyFrame` works out the sun, the ice depth, and the weather once per frame. `SkyViewSet` holds the four views and their blends. The renderer is split into `SkyBuilder` (no GL), `SkyTextures`, and `SkyGlPass`, and draws without allocating. The enum `SkySetting` lists every setting once, and `texelDegrees` no longer rebuilds the tables. `BandRows`, `SkyDisplay`, and `SkyRaySteps.ICE_BAND_STEPS` replace duplicated code. Array textures were considered and dropped by empireu. The screenshot harness stays, gated with the whole command as described under Decisions.
7. In progress since `2026-11-15`: the remaining sky work, in five steps planned with empireu: a baseline timing of the sky and `SkyLight`, moonlight in the sky from the same tables as the sun, the sun pillar from the ice layer's plates, diamond dust drawn as glints in the air near the player (in sunlight, in moonlight, and around lamps, shaded by terrain), and stars and the moon dimmed by the ice with a dusk at vanilla's pace. Step 1 is done: the sky's draw is timed, and the frame holds a `SkyLight` that is always the sun. See [the diary entry](../diary/2026-11-15_13-07-00_calculated-sky-light-and-draw-timer.md). Step 2 is committed: the moon lights the sky from the same tables below a measured switch elevation. See [the diary entry](../diary/2026-11-15_13-54-24_calculated-sky-moonlight.md). After empireu's night session, its brightness is `0.0125`, pillars keep full strength under the moon, and the sun shows through snow as vanilla's moon does. See [the tuning entry](../diary/2026-11-15_15-29-34_calculated-sky-moonlight-tuning.md). Step 3 is done: the plates' mirrored light forms a sun pillar in the halo maps, with its share from a new path class `end_mirror` in the datasets. See [the diary entry](../diary/2026-11-15_18-02-44_calculated-sky-sun-pillar.md). Step 4a is done: diamond dust shows as falling glints near the eye in sunlight and moonlight, more of them on the halos, with lit time, size by distance, and fall speed as settings tuned by empireu in game. A first pass that twinkled in place read as blinking and was replaced. See [the first pass](../diary/2026-11-15_21-00-48_calculated-sky-dust-glints-first-pass.md) and [the falling glints](../diary/2026-11-15_22-44-27_calculated-sky-falling-glints.md). Step 4b is done in a simpler form than planned: glints show only in open air, where their crystals could have fallen by the `MOTION_BLOCKING` heightmap, so none show under roofs, glass, or trees, or in caves, and there is no shade toward the light, since the terrain shows none either. See [the open air](../diary/2026-11-16_17-55-00_calculated-sky-dust-open-air.md). Step 4c is done: torches and lamps light the plain share of glints by the block light where they are, filtered from a volume of `36` blocks around the eye, in vanilla's block light color, also at night, and empireu kept the default brightness after a look in game. See [the lamp glints](../diary/2026-11-16_18-30-00_calculated-sky-dust-lamp-glints.md). The first part of step 5 is done: vanilla's stars dim through the ice and against a lit sky, the moon sprite takes the sun's square's display at its place, and the night sky and the fog have a floor. For the dusk, empireu chose an exposure adapted to the sky's luminance, which is next and will replace the floor with airglow. See [the stars and the night floor](../diary/2026-11-16_19-20-00_calculated-sky-stars-moon-night-floor.md).
8. Later: depth-based aerial perspective, clouds, ice and density that follow weather, and lore events such as swarms of narrow particle sizes, which turn the sky teal, lilac, or yellow-green offline.

Open tuning questions: the low sun is dim compared with vanilla, a halo on every clear day may be too much, and a day before snow stays bright under a high sun, since gloom would need lower clouds.

## Validation

- `runGameTestServer -PgameTestNamespaces=frostedheart_calculated_sky` for the optics.
- In `runClient`, with the pack colour preview datapack, at a frozen time of day: screenshots and frame rate with the sky on and off, at sunrise, noon, sunset, and night, in clear weather and snow. Check sun and moon placement, GL state leaks (hand, HUD, clouds, weather, translucent blocks, infrared view, white curtain), resize, resource reload, graphics modes, other dimensions, fluids, and blindness.
- Target for the draw: under `1 ms` per frame at 1080p.
- From `2026-11-07`, `/calculatedSky screenshots <set>` takes these comparisons from a set of poses, times, weather, and sky variants, and the contributor's `ScreenshotGrid --manifest` lays them out.
